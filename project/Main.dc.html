01 — Two signals

Steps and heart rate describe effort. Temen describes cost.

BrainFlow

Relative index

A continuous proxy for blood flow to the brain, read by light through the skin over the superficial temporal artery. It is a relative signal — it tells you whether flow is rising or falling against your own normal, not an absolute millilitres-per-minute figure.

  • →See how posture, breathing and position change supply to your head through the day.
  • →Catch the moments a wrist misreads — when heart rate and brain flow move in opposite directions.

Entropy

1 – 250 · every second

A live score for how hard your body is working to shed heat — the running cost of keeping you alive. At rest it sums the load across systems you rarely think about together: thermal, immune, digestive, cognitive, emotional, postural, recovery, even what you've taken that day.

02 — Why the temple

The wrist is a long way from the organ that matters most.

The idea that started it. Temen grew out of a research question: does a lifetime of standing upright slowly reduce blood flow to the brain? That hypothesis is still being tested and openly disputed by some clinicians. The sensor is useful either way — brain blood flow is already studied as a marker of ageing and cognition.

What it does not prove yet

  • Absolute brain blood flow — BrainFlow is a relative, normalised index.
  • Anything about disease. Every study so far used healthy adults.
  • Entropy as an independent biomarker — its benchmark is internal and unpublished.
  • Peer review — both BrainFlow papers are preprints.

Test it yourself

Qualified researchers can borrow devices to run independent studies on BrainFlow and Entropy. Results get shared whether they flatter us or not.
